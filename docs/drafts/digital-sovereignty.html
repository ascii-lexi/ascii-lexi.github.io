Digital Sovereignty

Many of you reading this, I'm sure, are familiar with Richard Stallman or other open source advocates. I am trying to avoid rehashing (nor salting) their arguments or ideals.

My goal is simple: define why I care about being digitally sovereignty, without diving into open source licensing. My second goal is more simple: learn to spell sovereignty by writing it [# numberofmentions] times.

Data is Data, Not a Permission Slip

In my experience, the data that is stored on device and shuffled around user networks is usually media.

Pictures, Videos, Books, Music, TV Shows, Movies.

This small category breadth is made up in its immense depth of each type of content. I don't think I need to illustrate how deeply attached people are to their media. Look at the immense price tags people are willing to pay for hard drive recovery. (admittedly less so since the advent of constant on-by-default cloud backups)

My point being, this data that people hold close to their heart, should not be held on some (often times adversarial) companies servers. Everyone who has used Netflix before the Cambrian Explosion of Streaming Based IP Capitalization has experienced having your favourite show being rug pulled from under you during a re-watch. Or having each new phone upgrade come with a phone camera megapixle doubling, forcing you to pay for more cloud storage to keep you child's graduation photos (see my other blog post about that)
